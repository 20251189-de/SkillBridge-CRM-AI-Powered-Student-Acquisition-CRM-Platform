## 1. Executive Summary

This project explores how an AI-supported Student Acquisition and Customer Relationship Management (CRM) system can help professional skill development institutes manage student enquiries and improve their admission process.

The project uses **SkillBridge Academy**, a hypothetical institute offering professional courses, as its business case. It was completed through three connected tasks:

1. **Business research using NotebookLM:** Study the student acquisition and admission process, identify operational problems, and develop research-based recommendations.
2. **AI Admission & CRM Assistant using Gemini Gems:** Configure a conversational AI assistant to support admission-related work.
3. **CRM application prototype using Google AI Studio:** Develop a prototype concept for managing enquiries, leads, student information, and admission performance.

Together, these tasks cover the business problem, an AI assistant, and an application-level solution.

## 2. Project Background

Professional skill development institutes receive enquiries through channels such as websites, social media, messaging platforms, phone calls, and walk-ins. Their admission teams need to record each enquiry, understand the student's requirements, assign follow-up responsibility, share course information, and track the student's decision.

When these activities rely on disconnected spreadsheets, manual messages, or inconsistent follow-up practices, institutes can lose track of prospective students. Counsellors may also find it difficult to identify priority leads and understand the status of the admission pipeline.

The project examines how a structured CRM workflow, supported by AI, could address these challenges.

> **Case-study note:** SkillBridge Academy is a hypothetical institute used for this project. The business scenario is illustrative and should not be interpreted as verified operational data from a real institution.

## 3. Objectives

- Understand the student enquiry-to-admission process in a skill development institute.

## 5. Proposed End-to-End Business Workflow

The three tasks support the following high-level process:

1. A prospective student submits an enquiry through an institute channel.
2. The enquiry is recorded in the CRM.
3. The lead is assigned to a counsellor.
4. The counsellor reviews the student's course interest and requirements.
5. The counsellor contacts the student and records the interaction.
6. The lead moves through the defined pipeline as the student progresses.
7. Follow-up activities are scheduled or marked for later action.
8. The student completes the admission process, remains in follow-up, or is marked as not converted.
9. Management reviews lead and admission reports to understand performance.

The exact stages and rules should follow the workflow specification included in the repository.

If implemented and adopted successfully, the proposed system could help an institute:

## 10. Limitations

- The business case uses a hypothetical institute and illustrative processes.
- Research findings depend on the documents and sources included in the project.
- An AI assistant can produce incorrect or incomplete responses; staff should review important information before sharing it with students.
- A prototype does not automatically demonstrate production readiness, security, scalability, or reliability.
- Integrations such as live WhatsApp messaging, database synchronisation, and automated notifications require separate implementation and testing if they are part of the intended final product.
- The project does not claim measured business outcomes unless supported by actual test or operational data.
